Beneath the number · a category the data proposed

The same word, different faiths

Left to sort themselves, the fragments refuse to let "religion" stay one thing. It splits by tradition, so cultures don't simply hold more or less faith. They hold different kinds. Arabic's is a faith of submission and the life to come; the Latin Catholic world's is a faith of trust and thanks. The map's single bar was hiding this.

The register · same question, different room

Change the room, change the answer

The same question, in English, asked three ways. When saying it costs something, like a comment under a hospice video, people reach for family and faith. Thinking out loud among engineers, they reach for the work of their hands. In open debate, for philosophy. Pick a room and watch the meaning of life rearrange itself.

Lexical, English only. The “argued” room leans religious partly by source selection (faith Q&A sites were sampled), so read it lightly. The clean, unconfounded signal is confessed vs. reflected: faith and family collapse, craft and growth rise.

The frame

What this is, and what it isn't

Every survey about meaning asks people directly, which is why surveys catch performances. This catches people saying it unprompted, at the moment it actually mattered, in their own language. That's not a bigger survey; it's a different kind of evidence.

It is also imperfect, and honest about it. The tagging is machine-assisted and hand-validated, not flawless; the crowds are self-selected; a keyword is not a soul. The reason to trust the map anyway is that it keeps reproducing things it was never told: real-world religiosity, the timing of a pandemic, the launch of a chatbot, all from nothing but raw, dated, anonymous text.
